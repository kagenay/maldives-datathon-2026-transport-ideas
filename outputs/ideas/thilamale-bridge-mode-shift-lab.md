# Thilamalé Bridge Mode Shift Lab

**Pitch.** Compare plausible bus, motorcycle and walking access after the planned Malé–Vilimalé–Gulhifalhu–Thilafushi road link opens.

**Type:** Adapted

**Research status:** 24 September 2026; concept document. Bridge opening to public traffic and exact timing are **not verified**.

## Problem, users and decision

The bridge could change road demand, bus access and walking patterns across Greater Malé. City and transport planners need a baseline and transparent scenarios before deciding on bus routes and junction works.

**Decision supported:** Which bus connection or bridge-approach safety investment should be tested first?

## Precedent and Maldivian equivalent

**Foreign precedent:** Transport for London's [WebCAT](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/urban-planning-and-construction/planning-applications/planning-with-webcat?intcmp=25861) compares current and future network accessibility for planning; this adapts that scenario method to a new island road link.

**Maldives evidence and confidence:** The [President's Office](https://presidency.gov.mv/Press/Article/37257) reported the Malé–Vilimalé span physically connected in August 2026 and described the full project corridor. The [transport ministry's planning terms](https://www.transport.gov.mv/pdf_file/741dcae8-041f-4442-9ee5-79f164737804/TOR-and-Timeline.pdf) explicitly request assessment of transport modes on the future link. Whether a public bridge-impact dashboard already exists is **not verified**. Confidence: high on the project and planning question; low on product status.

## Prototype interaction

Map present road/bus access and a separately styled **future-network scenario**. Select a new bus line, frequency or walking connection; compare reachable population, journey time and junction pressure at candidate bridge approaches. The bridge link is never shown as currently open.

## Data plan

**Available now / collectable by the team:** [Census 2022](https://statisticsmaldives.gov.mv/census-2022-results-summary/) population by island, dated [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/) roads and paths, published [RTL](https://www.rtl.mv/) bus stops and schedules, official project geography, and small manual 15-minute traffic/pedestrian counts at safe existing locations. Pilot coverage: Malé, Hulhumalé and Vilimalé; census fixed, network/schedules rechecked before demo, counts one-off.

**Future data to collect and method:** City and operator should log bridge link opening/status, approach turning counts by vehicle class and direction, hourly bus boardings/alightings by stop and trip, bus travel time, and pedestrian counts with fixed counters or repeat manual surveys. Baseline before opening; daily counts for the first months after opening, then quarterly comparisons. Publish aggregates only.

## Demo and rollout

**Datathon demo:** Show a real baseline map and two clearly labelled **hypothetical bridge-opening scenarios**. Compare one bus route and one safer walking connection. Do not present modeled demand or opening date as observed fact.

**Longer-term rollout:** Lock a pre-opening baseline, repeat counts after opening, recalibrate route and mode-share assumptions, and publish before/after evaluation.

## Value and safeguards

**Expected benefit:** Helps avoid car-only growth and prioritize inclusive bridge access.

**Limits:** Route choice and traffic response cannot be predicted reliably without observed demand; construction layout may change.

**Privacy:** Counts only; no plate or device traces.

**Dependencies:** Confirmed bridge design/opening status, safe count sites and operator schedule data.

## Datathon fit and ratings

The [2026 criteria](https://www.datathon.stats.gov.mv/faq) reward relevance, data use, innovation, execution, clarity and impact. This is directly tied to the Greater Malé transport-data question and offers a clear two-map, one-decision pitch.

**Feasibility: High — public baseline data and a small real count pilot are available; future demand is explicitly modeled.**

**Distinctiveness: Very high — the coming road-network change creates a timely local planning decision.**
