# Safe School Arrival Streets

**Pitch.** Rank school frontage crossings for safer walking and traffic management at drop-off time.

**Type:** Adapted  
**Research status:** 23 September 2026; concept document. Comparable Maldivian implementation status is assessed below.

## Problem, users and decision

School start creates localized pedestrian and motorcycle conflicts. City councils, schools and parents need a measured priority list.

**Decision supported:** Where should the first temporary school-street or protected crossing pilot go?

## Precedent and Maldivian equivalent

**Foreign precedent:** New York City’s [Vision Zero map](https://www.nyc.gov/content/visionzero/pages/open-data) includes safety interventions and schools; NYC DOT’s [Safe Routes to School work](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2005/pr05_39.shtml) created neighborhood traffic safety maps.

**Maldives evidence and confidence:** [Police](https://www.police.gov.mv/crime-statistics) publishes accident counts and the [transport plan terms](https://www.transport.gov.mv/pdf_file/741dcae8-041f-4442-9ee5-79f164737804/TOR-and-Timeline.pdf) seek traffic-safety improvements. A publicly verified school-arrival crossing priority dashboard is **not verified**. Confidence: high on those inputs; low on gap.

## Prototype interaction

One-school walking catchment with counted conflict points and alternative gate/arrival-time scenarios; map shows which children’s routes cross each point.

## Data plan

**Available now / collectable by the team:** [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/) paths, publicly located school entrance confirmed on site, 20-minute team counts of pedestrians and turns with no images retained, and [Census](https://statisticsmaldives.gov.mv/census-2022-results-summary/) area population context. One or two schools, one day.

**Future data to collect and method:** Education/city agencies collect anonymized home-zone (coarse), mode, arrival window, school gate, crossing point and near-miss category via opt-in annual survey plus repeated termly counts.

**Geographic coverage:** Pilot — one or two Greater Malé schools; later participating urban schools.

**Update frequency:** Census 2022 is a fixed release; where used, take a dated [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/) snapshot. Recheck any public timetable, fare or alert immediately before presentation. Team observations are one-off pilot snapshots. The future collection cadence is specified above; no live feed is assumed for the demo.

Where a future input is required, any generated values in the datathon view must be marked **synthetic scenario data**, visually separated from observed/public data. The [official FAQ](https://www.datathon.stats.gov.mv/faq) allows public or newly gathered credited data, says MBS will provide Census 2022/HIES 2019 to participants, and also prohibits private/nonpublic sources; confirm the scope and terms of event-provided files before using them.

## Demo and rollout

**Datathon demo:** Use actual, clearly limited observation windows; simulate a gate change with assumptions rather than promise casualty reduction.

**Longer-term rollout:** Co-design a reversible street trial with schools and repeat counts before and after.

## Value and safeguards

**Expected benefit:** Protects children and supports walking.

**Limits:** School-day sample and self-selection bias.

**Privacy:** Do not collect student names, exact homes, images or individual tracks.

**Dependencies:** School permission and safe observation.

## Datathon fit and ratings

The [2026 theme and criteria](https://www.datathon.stats.gov.mv/faq) emphasize transport data systems, GIS, decision-making, relevance, data use, innovation, technical execution, clarity, impact and teamwork. **Fit:** Concrete safety decision, local data and clear before/after visual.

**Feasibility: High with school access; otherwise use public-side counts only.**  
**Distinctiveness: Medium — known approach tailored to dense island streets.**
