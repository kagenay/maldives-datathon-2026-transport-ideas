# Maldives Datathon 2026 transport ideas

Research checked 23 September 2026. The [event home](https://www.datathon.stats.gov.mv/) states the theme, **“Mapping Mobility through Data: Innovating Transport Insights for a Connected Maldives,”** and schedules the event for 24 October 2026 in Malé. The [2026 honours page](https://www.datathon.stats.gov.mv/honours/2026) confirms the theme and Greater Malé emphasis but says no honours data are available for this edition yet. The [FAQ](https://www.datathon.stats.gov.mv/faq) gives a 27 September 2026 registration deadline, teams of 3–5, a 7-minute pitch plus 5-minute Q&A, submission of code/documentation/presentation materials, and judging on relevance, data utilization, innovation, technical execution, clarity, impact and teamwork. The FAQ gives no numeric weights for those criteria. It calls the event 10 hours while listing 09:00–20:00, so confirm the working schedule. The scope emphasizes Greater Malé while explicitly including marine, road, pedestrian, inter-island, sustainable and emergency mobility. The FAQ says MBS will provide Census 2022/HIES 2019 data, but also says private or nonpublic data are prohibited. **Confirm with organizers how event-provided files may be used; do not assume unpublished operator or patient data are allowed.**

The strongest five to investigate first are **#1 Island-to-Clinic Access Atlas**, **#2 Missed-Connection Ferry Heatmap**, **#3 Emergency Referral Travel Stress Test**, **#4 Accessible Journey Chain Audit**, and **#5 Weather-Resilient Sailing Windows**. They each have a single clear planning decision and a strong map-led story for seven minutes. #1 and #2 can start with public schedules and census; #4 can collect a small real field pilot; #3 and #5 are compelling scenarios whose unobserved inputs must be prominently synthetic. Confirm route data reuse and clinic/capability facts before promising a live product.

## Numbered idea list

1. [Island-to-Clinic Access Atlas](island-to-clinic-access-atlas.md) — Map the actual scheduled time and transfer burden for each island to reach a higher-level health facility.
2. [Missed-Connection Ferry Heatmap](missed-connection-ferry-heatmap.md) — Find timetable transfers that are possible on paper but leave passengers with an unsafe or very long wait.
3. [Emergency Referral Travel Stress Test](emergency-referral-travel-stress-test.md) — Stress-test which islands lack a timely route to appropriate care when one sea or air link is unavailable.
4. [Accessible Journey Chain Audit](accessible-journey-chain-audit.md) — Identify where a nominally accessible journey fails between sidewalk, terminal, gangway, vessel and clinic.
5. [Weather-Resilient Sailing Windows](weather-resilient-sailing-windows.md) — Show which scheduled connections are exposed to official marine alerts and how alternate sailing windows could preserve access.
6. [Street Safety Intervention Map](street-safety-intervention-map.md) — Prioritize Greater Malé junctions by exposure, observed conflicts and feasible street changes.
7. [After-Dark Island Access Gap](after-dark-island-access-gap.md) — Reveal which islands lose practical access to essential destinations after the last scheduled sailing.
8. [Ferry Reliability and Lifeline Scorecard](ferry-reliability-and-lifeline-scorecard.md) — Compare route reliability and the population affected when an essential sailing fails.
9. [Greater Malé Traffic Count Map](greater-male-traffic-count-map.md) — Create repeatable junction-level vehicle counts to target congestion measures instead of relying on anecdote.
10. [Open Multimodal Island Journey Graph](open-multimodal-island-journey-graph.md) — Represent public bus, ferry and walking legs in one transparent graph for route-change testing.
11. [Boat and Bus Crowding Pulse](boat-and-bus-crowding-pulse.md) — Show where crowding occurs by departure and whether shifting frequency or capacity would reduce it.
12. [Demand-Responsive Atoll Shuttle Testbed](demand-responsive-atoll-shuttle-testbed.md) — Test whether a small pre-booked speedboat service could fill gaps without replacing viable fixed routes.
13. [Safe School Arrival Streets](safe-school-arrival-streets.md) — Rank school frontage crossings for safer walking and traffic management at drop-off time.
14. [Single-Link Failure Atlas](single-link-failure-atlas.md) — Identify the bridges, wharves and ferry legs whose closure disconnects the most people from essentials.
15. [Ferry-to-Bus Transfer Walk Clock](ferry-to-bus-transfer-walk-clock.md) — Measure whether the physical walk from ferry arrival to bus boarding fits the published transfer window.
16. [Rain-Flood Walking Detour Atlas](rain-flood-walking-detour-atlas.md) — Show how localized flooding changes safe walking access to ferry terminals, clinics and bus stops.
17. [Small-Ferry Fuel and Emissions Ledger](small-ferry-fuel-and-emissions-ledger.md) — Compare route-level fuel intensity and schedule changes for a lower-emission public ferry fleet.
18. [Berth Turnaround Bottleneck Map](berth-turnaround-bottleneck-map.md) — Measure where berth occupancy and boarding time force ferries or speedboats to queue.
19. [Island Evacuation Pickup Planner](island-evacuation-pickup-planner.md) — Compare evacuation pickup sites and vessel allocation for isolated islands under a disaster scenario.
20. [Harbour Crossing Conflict Diary](harbour-crossing-conflict-diary.md) — Locate recurring conflicts among pedestrians, motorcycles, cargo carts and boarding queues near ferry terminals.
21. [Essential Medicines Parcel Reach](essential-medicines-parcel-reach.md) — Test how ferry schedules affect the time to move essential medicine parcels to island clinics.
22. [Holiday Last-Seat Stress Test](holiday-last-seat-stress-test.md) — Estimate how peak holiday demand could strand travelers even when a timetable exists.
23. [Island Fare Burden Lens](island-fare-burden-lens.md) — Compare the affordability of reaching essential services across islands and household types.
24. [Heat-and-Rain Walking Comfort Map](heat-and-rain-walking-comfort-map.md) — Locate exposed walks to bus stops and ferry terminals where shade or shelter would help the most people.
25. [Port-to-Street Delivery Window Planner](port-to-street-delivery-window-planner.md) — Test how changing cargo unloading windows could reduce conflicts with peak walking and bus movement.

## Source and interpretation notes

- [MBS Census 2022 summary tables](https://statisticsmaldives.gov.mv/census-2022-results-summary/) and [island indicators](https://statisticsmaldives.gov.mv/census-2022-island-and-atoll-level-indicator-sheets/) are public starting points; they are not trip-demand or passenger-flow datasets. [MBS OneMap](https://statisticsmaldives.gov.mv/atoll-profiles/) already maps census topics.
- [RTL](https://www.rtl.mv/) already supplies routes, schedules, live tracking and tickets. [MTCC’s 2023 report](https://mtcc.mv/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Annual-Report-2023-2.pdf) describes dynamic ferry schedules and app tracking. Ideas here should complement those functions. The availability of a public machine-readable feed was **not verified**.
- [Maldives Police crime statistics](https://www.police.gov.mv/crime-statistics) provide traffic-accident aggregates; these cannot be plotted as crash points. [MMS](https://www.meteorology.gov.mv/forecast) supplies marine forecasts and [alert definitions](https://meteorology.gov.mv/awareness), but those do not substitute for operator decisions or vessel-specific safety guidance.
- “Not verified” means this research did not establish whether an equivalent exists. It is not a claim of absence. Public schedule terms, service coverage, geography, HIES fields and event data permissions should be rechecked immediately before choosing a build.
